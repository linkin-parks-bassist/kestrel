effects/ contains seventeen current-image descriptors plus older repository examples. The foundational set is LEVEL, INVPHASE, CLIP, CUBEDRV, OCTAVE, SVFLP/SVFHP/SVFBP/SVFNOTCH, SVFLP4 and SVFTONE. The further experiments are WAH, VOWEL, OCTFUZZ, DRIVE, GROWL and BASSRING. All filenames fit carrier 8.3 FAT. The library uses arithmetic, private-state Chamberlin SVF, built-in LUTs and scratchpad state, never the old general filter engine. effects/README.md owns controls and public usage.

BASSRING (Bass Ring) multiplies input by a free-running sine carrier, retaining phase in scratchpad memory across samples. Carrier is nominally 20–220 Hz, with dry/wet Mix. Generic arithmetic wraps phase; there is no compiler special case. Q15 increment quantization gives roughly 1.35-Hz frequency steps at 44.1 kHz, so this is not an exactly tuned or pitch-tracking oscillator. Its 13 blocks take at most 150 cycles/sample at default settings.

GROWL (Cross Growl) multiplies driven 400-Hz low-pass and 1200-Hz band-pass components, boosts the product, then uses the low component to move a third SVF cutoff on every sample within 100–3000-Hz equivalents. Controls are Excitation, Audio Motion and Mix; the final wet coefficient is 3*Mix. WAH is a manually swept damping-normalized resonant band-pass. VOWEL moves two band-pass centers from 350/2200 Hz to 800/1200 Hz, without an envelope follower, LFO or voice model. OCTFUZZ rectifies, removes DC and saturates before low-pass tone; DRIVE applies 1.5x−0.5x³ and tone filtering. Rectification does not pitch-shift arbitrary chords.

The SVF family uses signed-Q15 cutoff 2*sin(pi*cutoff/sample_rate) with independently compensated damping and conservative declared ranges. Resonant/loud signals can clip; nonlinear effects lack oversampling and can alias. SVFTONE's middle is a notch through low/high phase cancellation, not flat-center EQ.

The compiler/sample-model/actual-core loop has checked 89 default/control-corner programs and 1339524 exact outputs: 84 programs in /tmp/kestrel-effect-library/results.json and five in /tmp/kestrel-bassring/results.json. Defaults use 44100 samples, corners 8192, with endpoints, impulse, step and tones. Selected response, identity/polarity and clipping checks pass; maximum default costs range 18–150 cycles/sample. GROWL and BASSRING preserve dry samples exactly at Mix=0. This is execution evidence, not sonic approval.

BASSRING additionally matches 132300 synthetic low-B/low-E/open-A pluck samples exactly. Its nominal 55-Hz carrier measures 55.181 Hz. Full-wet low E at 41.2034 Hz yields roughly 4103-code sum/difference components at 13.977/96.384 Hz and 17 codes at the original fundamental. Silence stays zero. /tmp/kestrel-bassring-audition/comparison.json and its WAVs record these checks; tools/test_eff_state.py retains a sustained-carrier regression. how/to/verify/an/effect/descriptor.md owns workflow and exclusions.

David plays bass guitar exclusively: audition fundamentals, harmonics and playing transients accordingly. He finds WAH/VOWEL barely noticeable and OCTFUZZ/DRIVE merely distortion flavors. Cross Growl sounds like high-pass plus distortion with occasional nonmusical gain changes; its split and gain behavior require reconsideration. None of those experiments has met his request for interesting, musically useful transformations. Bass Ring listening acceptance is pending. David wants the device's arbitrary audio-rate DSP exploited through cross-modulation, dynamically driven filters, stateful/nonlinear feedback and modulation/time effects.

Older GAIN, TD, EF, HPF, LPF and 3BEQ examples are outside the verified batch. General-filter instructions require ENABLE_FILTER, excluded from the installed default; firmware capability rejection/lowering is not implemented. David says not to use that engine for this library, though a larger 24-bit FPGA may justify revisiting it. Fractional delay is future work. He likes current flanging; long-delay modulation remains permitted but discouraged because of quantization.
