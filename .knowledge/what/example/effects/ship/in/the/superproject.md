effects/ contains sixteen current-image descriptors plus older repository examples. GROWL (Cross Growl) multiplies driven 400-Hz low-pass and 1200-Hz band-pass components, boosts the product, and sends it through a third SVF whose cutoff is moved by the low component on every sample. Its coefficient is bounded to 100–3000-Hz equivalents; controls are Excitation, Audio Motion and dry/wet Mix. The final wet coefficient is 3*Mix to compensate attenuation. Other additions are WAH (resonant swept band-pass), VOWEL (two moving formants), OCTFUZZ (rectification, DC cleanup, saturating drive and low-pass tone), and DRIVE (cubic drive followed by low-pass tone). The foundational batch is LEVEL, INVPHASE, CLIP, CUBEDRV, OCTAVE, SVFLP/SVFHP/SVFBP/SVFNOTCH, SVFLP4 and SVFTONE. All use arithmetic and private-state Chamberlin SVF, never the general filter engine. Filenames fit carrier 8.3 FAT; controls and usage are in effects/README.md.

WAH is manually swept, with damping-normalized band output. VOWEL moves centers from 350/2200 Hz to 800/1200 Hz as Mouth moves 0–1; these are approximate vowel-like colors, without voice modeling, an LFO or an envelope follower. OCTFUZZ emphasizes rectifier harmonics, rather than pitch-shifting arbitrary chords. DRIVE shapes a saturated input with 1.5x−0.5x³ then filters it.

The SVF family uses signed-Q15 cutoff 2*sin(pi*cutoff/sample_rate) and independently compensated damping. Conservative declared ranges are part of each descriptor. Resonant/loud inputs can clip. SVFTONE's middle creates a notch because low/high cancel near the split; it is not a flat-center EQ. Nonlinear effects lack oversampling and can alias.

The production compiler/sample-model/actual-core loop passes 84 default/control-corner programs, totaling 1262656 exact output comparisons. Defaults render 44100 samples and corners 8192, with signed endpoints, impulse, step and tones. Selected filter responses, unity/polarity and clipping ceilings are checked. Maximum default costs are 18–126 cycles/sample; WAH/VOWEL/OCTFUZZ/DRIVE take 39/61/92/101; GROWL takes 126. GROWL additionally verifies that Mix=0 preserves each input sample exactly. Two 132300-sample synthetic plucked-note renders also match the RTL exactly: Audio Motion=1 versus 0 gives a 4686.57-code RMS difference; the moving render has dry/wet RMS 1902.75/2453.03 and peak 18797. /tmp/kestrel-growl-audition/comparison.json and moving/fixed/dry-wet.wav own that retained audition evidence; this establishes a substantial numerical modulation effect for this input, not listening approval. This live selection was not an automated activation test of every addition. David's listening feedback is that WAH and VOWEL are barely noticeable, while OCTFUZZ and DRIVE are merely flavors of distortion. These four do not satisfy his request for more interesting effects. Numerical execution checks remain valid; sonic usefulness and perceptual strength are separate acceptance criteria. David wants to exploit sample-by-sample arbitrary DSP: audio-rate cross-modulation, dynamically driven filters and stateful feedback are appropriate exploration directions alongside modulation/time effects. GROWL is the first cross-modulation experiment; listening acceptance is still open. Source: David's direct feedback on the deployed additions.

Older repository GAIN, TD, EF, HPF, LPF and 3BEQ examples are outside this batch. General-filter instructions need ENABLE_FILTER, excluded from the installed default; capability-aware rejection/lowering is not implemented. David says not to use that engine for the library, though he may revisit it on a larger FPGA with 24-bit audio. Fractional delay remains future work; current flanging sounds good to him, while long-delay modulation is permitted but discouraged because of quantization.
